# Bor — The Vestige Bell and the Spellplague Choice

**Status:** Canon  
**Continuity:** Rolling High Club  
**Source status:** Explicit DM canon promotion, 12-08-2026  
**Visibility:** NON_META as shared Bor / The Twelve history. This does not make the details automatically known to outsiders.

## Canon Event

During the Spellplague, **all Twelve were together on the same ship** when the Vestige Bell connected to Tymora activated a divine condition.

The condition was simple and irreversible in the form Bor remembers it:

- if Bor **left the Vestige Bell behind in order to save someone who needed help**, the Bell would not return to him;
- instead, the sacrifice would grant a blessing.

Bor chose the Twelve and left the Vestige Bell behind.

The important part of the event is not that Bor failed to keep the Bell. He consciously chose his people over the object when keeping both was no longer possible.

## The Blessing

The Twelve did **not** become immortal.

Bor describes the lasting effect in practical family terms: **one year for them corresponds to roughly 250 years for the rest of the world**. This ratio is the established RHC shorthand for the blessing's effect; this canon milestone does not further define its metaphysical mechanism.

The blessing does not remove every aging problem or make later age-altering magic irrelevant.

- **Nathan** knows how to prepare **Potions of Longevity** when they are needed.
- **Trevor** once drank one and aged **11 years**, reaching the age of **42**.
- This became a Family joke: Trevor, at forty-two, is treated as the group's 'wise old man'. He does not carry the title with particular dignity.

## The Unspoken Contract of the Twelve

Bor later described what followed the Spellplague choice as if all Twelve had signed a contract without consciously realizing it.

No literal contract was made.

The durable truth is the relationship it describes:

- each member covers the others when something is missing;
- each member contributes what the others cannot;
- mutual protection and complementary roles became instinctive rather than negotiated;
- over the centuries, this developed into the Family-level trust that defines the Twelve.

For Bor, the defining memory is therefore not simply 'the day he lost a Bell'. It is the day the choice between the Bell and his people made visible what the Twelve would become to one another.

## Why Bor Cares About Bells

Bor's fascination with bells remains funny in play, but it has a serious root.

To him, bells function as both:

1. **an alarm** — a symbol that something may happen and someone may need him;
2. **a reminder** — when the choice matters, people come before possessions.

This meaning does not remove the recurring comedy of Bor acquiring, carrying or taking bells extremely seriously. The comedy and the history coexist.

## Canon Boundaries

- The **Vestige Bell** is distinct from Bor's small silver **Bell of the Lady's Smile**.
- This record does not establish a new present-day location for the lost Vestige Bell.
- This record does not establish that the Vestige Bell can now be recovered, recreated or made to return.
- The blessing described here is a specific RHC canon event, not a general D&D rule or a reusable magic-item mechanic.
- No additional Spellplague scene details are canon merely because they appeared in improvisation. Only the facts preserved in this record were explicitly promoted by the DM.

## Character Meaning

The event explains three durable parts of Bor:

- why the Twelve are not merely companions but Family;
- why Bor's instinct is to protect his people before property or status;
- why a bell can be simultaneously ridiculous, sacred, practical and emotionally important to him.
